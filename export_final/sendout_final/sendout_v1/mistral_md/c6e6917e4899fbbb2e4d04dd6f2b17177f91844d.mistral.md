A glance at this table will show that, in quinquennial periods, the age returns are almost free from inaccuracies. In every country where ages are correctly returned, it is found that the numbers under each quinquennial period decrease gradually with advancing age, and in this table the gradation is uniform except in one place. Persons between 25 and 29 years of age are numerically greater than those between 20 and 24, while for the rest the decrease is uniformly gradual. This anomaly may be the result of erroneous returns, but it may probably be due, at least in part, to the loss of human fertility in 1866—67, when there was in Cochin distress and scarcity perilously bordering on famine.

In this method of grouping of the population, we see that the numbers decrease pretty steadily with each advancing period of age and this fact shows that the errors are those of ignorance in regard to exact age, rather than of wilful misrepresentation, so that the age-returns when grouped into decennial periods may be taken as almost correct.
