There are 20,470 blind persons in the Province, of whom 18,567 are in British districts and 1,903 in the Feudatory States. In other words, there is one person blind in every 632 of the population, being one in 581 in British territory and one in 1,135 in the Feudatories. As in the case of the infirmities before noticed, the returns

of the present Census show a remarkable falling off, as compared with those of ten years ago. There are now three blind persons for every four there were in 1881; in 1872 there were only two on a like proportion. There can ordinarily be little mistake about the enumeration of the blind, so the decrease in their number at the present Census as compared with that preceding—a decrease which is common to other provinces as well as this—must be looked upon as a satisfactory result of the spread of vaccination, and of the greater readiness of the people to avail themselves of medical treatment for the infirmity.
