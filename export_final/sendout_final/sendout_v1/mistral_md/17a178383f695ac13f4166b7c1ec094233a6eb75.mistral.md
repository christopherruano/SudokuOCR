However, it should be borne in mind that the figures of unmarried persons at a given time, have no direct bearing on the universality of marriage, as most of the persons now shown as unmarried will marry at a later age period. From the standpoint of the universality of marriage, it will be noticed that among Aroras and Kashmiri Pandits the column for unmarried females is entirely blank at the last age period (40 and over), while in the case of other Hindu castes it has a very limited range between 1 and 5 per mille. Turning to the same column for males, Kashmiri Pandits show the largest proportion of 163 unmarried men, compared to 138 among Thakkars, 111 among Khatries and 104 among Rajputs. So far as the higher castes are concerned, the higher incidence of unmarried persons generally corresponds with the lower numerical proportion of their females. Looking at the Mohammedan castes from the same point of view, we find that the proportion of unmarried females at the last age period varies for important castes from 5 to 12 per mille, while the figures for the males of the same age for main tribes range between 12 and 40 per mille and are much below those of the important Hindu castes. Even the unmarried Mohammedan Rajputs are only 30 compared to 104 Hindu Rajputs. Only Bafindas and Pathans show comparatively higher figures of 59 and 88, but their population is too small to deserve serious consideration. This comparatively low incidence of unmarried males among Mohammedans is mainly due to the widows being also available for marriage. It will be further noticed that the proportion of widows of all ages for Mohammedans varies from 93 to 126, while the figures for Hindu widows range between 145 and 250 per mille. The Mohammedan Jats are, however, an exception to the rule, giving a proportion of 145, which is exactly the same as for their Hindu brethren.

In regard to the different age-periods for males, there is no perceptible difference in the figures for males in the two caste groups. The proportion of married men is generally very small in the two early age-periods, varying generally from 1 to 5 per mille. The Arains only show comparatively higher figures (15 per mille at 5-12), but the tribe is unimportant. Roughly speaking, about 1/3rd of the married males are married at 12-20 and about 2/3rd after the age of 20.

Similarly, there is no marked divergence in females of the 0-5 age group, the number of married girls in each group being generally near four or five per mille. But the difference becomes distinctly noticeable when we come to the age group 5-12, which generally shows lower figures for Musalman tribes, the proportion for Musalman Jats and Rajputs being 38 and 68 respectively against 118 and 129 for the same tribes in the Hindu group. It is interesting to note that Kashmiri Pandits show the lowest figure (21) in the whole list, which is far below the proportion for Kashmiri Musalmans (58).

It is also apparent that the backward rural classes are generally in the forefront in respect of early marriage, the figures for Brahmans, Chamiars, Meghs, Thakkars and Gujjars ranging between 128 and 209 per mille. This is probably due mainly to the fact that marriage among the ordinary rural tribes is a much easier and simpler affair than among the higher castes, with its expensive ceremonials and social entertainments. A glance at the figures of the last Census will show that a higher proportion of marriages at 5-12 among large tribal groups is noticeable at the present Census. For instance, Chamiars give a proportion of 179 against 171, Meghs of 209 against 186, and Thakkars of 202 against 176. In these circumstances, I think it would be futile to draw any general conclusions from these fluctuations as to the attitude of the people, and the variations are generally caused by accidental causes, such as the nature of the harvests, climatic conditions, the presence or absence of epidemics and last but not the least, the auspicious or inauspicious character of certain months and years.
