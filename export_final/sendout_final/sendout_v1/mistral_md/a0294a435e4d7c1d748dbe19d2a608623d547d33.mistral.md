23. I will illustrate this by an extreme case. Suppose the average age at which females are married to be 15 years in India, and 20 years in England. Suppose that 10 females aged 15 are married in India, on the 1st January, 1815, and 10 females aged 20 are married on the same date in England. Suppose that at the end of 15 years each woman has had 15 daughters and has ceased bearing. On the 1st of January, 1831, therefore, the Indian wives would have 150 daughters and the English a similar number of whom none would be above 15 years of age. But supposing that all the Indian daughters were married at 15 and all the English at 20 and were all as prolific as their mothers what would be the result at a Census taken in 1845. The result is shown in the following Statement:—

|  ID AND ABOVE. |   | UNDER 15 YEARS.  |   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  30 | 40 | 50 | 60  |
|  YEAR. | 1 | 2 | 3  |
|  Indian, .. ... | 1 | 2 | 3  |
|  English, .. .. | 50 | 60 | 70  |

It will here be seen that in 1845 in both the number of adults is 160, while in the Indian portion there are 1,050 children under the age of 15 and only 450 in the English, i. e., to every 100 English children there are 233 Indian, or more than double.

24. Now this is as I have said an extreme case but the difference in the number of the children in the two countries is so very large that there seems little or no doubt that the principle would hold good, no matter how great may be the modifications in regard to number and sex of children.

25. The truth seems to be that the proportion of children under 15 to adults depends on the proportion of births to the total population and it will be found that in a country where women marry at 15, the proportion of births to population, in any given period, say 50 years, will always be greatly in excess of the proportion in countries where the marriage age is higher, and I believe that the differences will be so great that it will result in a very high proportion of children under 15, even in a country where infant mortality is excessive, where male births exceed female births and where the average age of the human race is low.

184. Since it is found that the proportion of children in Upper India closely approaches that of England, one of the methods of accounting for the circumstance would be to see what reasons are alleged for it in the English Census Reports, where the accuracy in the ascertainment of facts, and the soundness of conclusions drawn from them is so much greater than has ever been attained, or perhaps can ever be expected in India. But the desired information is not very accessible.

185. It is well-known that the English race is one of the most prolific in the world. It is also known that female adults largely exceed male adults in England. The percentage of married females in England, at the different ages, from 15 to 50, is not very different from that of France. In the ages from 30 upwards it is almost exactly the same. But in the lower ages (15 to 30) it is somewhat higher in France, that is, females in France marry earlier but do not live quite so long as females in England. As the percentage of women below the age of 40, or during the child-bearing period, is greater in France than in England the number of children should be greater, if all the conditions are the same. But the percentage of children is less in France than in England. The conditions are not the same.
