How to read this map. With historical borders, each colored shape is a district or princely state as tabulated in that census, and it carries that unit's own figures — whether it had its own table or only appears as a row inside a provincial one. Within present-day India each outline follows the 1941 Census of India district map (digitized by India State Stories, FLAME University): modern sub-districts (GADM 4.1) are assigned to the 1941 unit covering most of them, so the lines are drawn accurately while the composition is the 1941 map's; units smaller than a sub-district are drawn straight from the 1941 map (±15 km). Earlier years regroup the 1941 units using dated splits and renames (e.g. Guntur inside Kistna before 1904). Outside India (Pakistan, Bangladesh, Burma) outlines are reconstructed from modern successor districts. Boundary changes that never crossed a 1941 line are not visible. Modern borders show the same information on today's districts. Self-checked means the age bands add up to the printed total (or to 1,000/10,000 for proportional tables). On the indicator layers, units without a local figure show their province's value pale with a dashed outline. Scales are fixed across years.
